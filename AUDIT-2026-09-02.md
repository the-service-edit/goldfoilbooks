# Site audit, 2 September 2026

Eighteen pages, rendered at 320, 390, 768 and 1440px, plus the live
Squarespace store and the GitHub Pages prototype over the real network.

**Headline: the code is clean. The problems are in what has been uploaded.**
Zero JavaScript errors on any page at any width. Zero horizontal overflow at
any width. Every form control has an accessible name. Every image has alt
text. Markup balances on all 18 pages. The three most valuable findings are
all upload settings in Squarespace, not code.

---

## 1. CRITICAL — two of three product pages are 13x heavier than they need to be

Measured on the live CDN, same URL pattern, same requested size, same WebP
conversion, Chrome:

| Source upload | Delivered at 1500w |
|---|---|
| ACOTAR cover, uploaded as **.png** | **2,414 KB** |
| ACOMAF cover, uploaded as **.png** | **2,287 KB** |
| ACOWAR cover, uploaded as **.jpg** | **173 KB** |
| ACOMAF back cover, **.png** | 2,390 KB |
| ACOWAR back cover, **.jpg** | 191 KB |

The only variable is the format the file was uploaded in. Squarespace converts
everything to WebP, but a PNG source produces *lossless* WebP and a JPEG
source produces *lossy* WebP. Photographs uploaded as PNG therefore come out
roughly thirteen times larger for no visible gain.

The ACOMAF product page currently loads **2.3 MB for the hero image alone**,
before a single thing below the fold. On a phone on mobile data that is the
difference between a page that appears in about a second and one that takes
five to ten. This audience arrives from Instagram and TikTok, on phones, and
a slow first image is the most expensive kind of slow there is.

**Fix.** Re-upload the sixteen ACOTAR and ACOMAF product photographs as JPEG
at quality 80-85. ACOWAR is already correct and is the proof it works. Nothing
else changes: same photos, same CDN, same pages.

This is the single highest-return item in this document.

## 2. CRITICAL — the mobile menu could not be closed *(fixed)*

`.gfb-nav.is-open` is `position: fixed; z-index: 99` and covers the whole
viewport. `.gfb-burger` was `position: static`, so it painted underneath its
own overlay. `document.elementFromPoint` at the centre of the Close button
returned the nav, not the button.

In practice: open the menu on a phone, and the only ways out were following a
link or reloading. Escape closed it, which is no help on a phone.

Fixed by lifting the control above its overlay. Verified on all 18 pages at
390px: the Close button is now the top element and the menu closes.

## 3. HIGH — artwork is downloadable at full resolution *(settings)*

All eight artworks are uploaded at 1800 x 2700. `?format=original` on any
artwork URL returns the whole file. At 150dpi that prints to about 12 x 18
inches: a free version of a $7.99 product, reachable without a right click or
developer tools.

**Fix.** Re-upload capped at 1400px on the long edge and delete the 1800px
originals from the image manager. Covered in full in section 9 of
`SQUARESPACE-PRODUCT-PAGES.md`.

`data-protect` now covers all 178 images site-wide, but be clear that this
stops casual saving only. Upload resolution is the actual control.

---

## 4. HIGH — social previews are undersized, and the title is doubled

On the live product page:

- `twitter:card` is **`summary`**, the small square thumbnail. For a visual
  product sold to a visual-first audience, `summary_large_image` roughly
  doubles the visual footprint of every shared link. This is a Squarespace
  setting and it is close to free.
- The page title is **93 characters** and contains the brand twice:
  *"A Court of Thorns and Roses Handbound Collector's Edition | Gold Foil
  Books — GOLD FOIL BOOKS"*. Squarespace appends the site title automatically,
  so remove "| Gold Foil Books" from the product's own SEO title field. Google
  truncates around 60 characters, so at present the useful part is cut.
- `og:image` is served over `http://`, not `https://`.
- Two `<h1>` elements: the product name and the logo wordmark.
- Five of thirty-seven images have no alt text.

## 5. MEDIUM — the GitHub Pages copy is still competing with the real store

`robots.txt` returns nothing and there is no `noindex`. It remains a
near-complete duplicate of a live commercial site on an indexable domain.
Carried over from the first audit, still open. A `robots.txt` with
`Disallow: /` in the repo root fixes it in one file.

## 6. MEDIUM — contrast failure on all eight artwork pages *(fixed)*

`.gfb-detail-row figcaption` had a dark-ground override and `.gfb-figure
figcaption` did not, so every "Where it lives" caption rendered `--ink-mute`
`#6a6157` on `#12100e`: **3.13:1 at 11.5px**, a real AA failure. Now 16.99:1.

That was mine, introduced when the artwork pages were built.

---

## 7. LOW — metadata housekeeping

- 17 of 18 prototype pages have no canonical tag and no Open Graph tags. Only
  matters for the prototype, since Squarespace generates its own, but a
  canonical pointing at goldfoilbooks.com.au would also help with section 5.
- Eight page titles exceed 62 characters; twelve meta descriptions exceed 165.
- `product.html` was claiming `aggregateRating` 5.0 from 2 reviews in its
  structured data. The live product page makes no such claim. **Removed**, so
  the prototype cannot teach anyone to publish a rating that may sit on a
  different product record.

## Appendix: the Squarespace side, step by step

Everything below is admin work in Squarespace. None of it touches this repo,
none of it needs the redesign, and all of it improves the store as it stands
today. Roughly an afternoon.

### 1. Product photographs to JPEG *(the 13x win)*

`tools/photos-to-jpeg.command` converts a folder of PNGs to quality-82 JPEG
using `sips`, which is built into macOS. Double-click it, drag in the folder
of originals, and it writes a `jpeg` subfolder. Originals are never modified,
and it prints the before and after size of every file.

Then in Squarespace, for **A Court of Thorns and Roses** and **A Court of Mist
and Fury** only (Wings and Ruin is already JPEG and is the proof this works):

- Open the product, delete the eight PNG images, upload the eight JPEGs.
- Keep the same order: 1 Cover, 2 Spine, 3 Back, 4 Spine/Cover, 5 Edges/Cover,
  6 Edges/Back, 7 Front Endpaper, 8 Back Endpaper.
- Re-check the alt text on each; Squarespace does not carry it across.

Expect the hero to drop from about 2,300 KB to about 175 KB.

### 2. Artwork downloads capped at 1400px

For each of the eight artwork products: re-upload the display image at 1400px
on the long edge, then **delete the 1800px original from the image manager**.
Replacing a product image does not always purge the old asset, and while it is
on the CDN `?format=original` still returns it.

The print-resolution file goes only in the digital download attachment, never
as a product image.

### 3. Social preview and titles

- **Marketing > SEO / Social Sharing**: set the Twitter card type to
  `summary_large_image`. Currently `summary`, the small square thumbnail.
- Product **SEO panels**: use the table above. Do not type the brand, it is
  appended.
- Check the social image is served over `https://`; it is currently `http://`.

### 4. Inventory and waitlists

- **Commerce > Inventory**: set each rebind to the number of copies you can
  genuinely bind in one cycle. Unlimited stock means the waitlist can never
  appear, whatever the page markup says.
- Enable **Product Waitlists**. Signups collect under Commerce > Waitlists and
  are the only honest read you will get on demand per title.

### 5. Decide the series-lock mechanism

The disclosure on all three rebind pages is marked `CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING`
and currently reads *"Your code arrives with your dispatch email."* It now
backs a promise covering every book in the series with no end date. Either
build the code, or change the sentence to what you will actually do. Do not
publish it as it stands.

- Dark-ground figcaption contrast, 3.13:1 to 16.99:1.
